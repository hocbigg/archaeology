## Introduction

Archaeology investigates the human past through its surviving physical traces, from micro-artifacts and stratified sediments to monuments and regional landscapes. Written history covers only literate societies and leans toward administrative elites. Archaeology reaches the full time span of the human career and shows how technology, subsistence strategies, and social organization changed.

This curriculum is for self-directed learners with no background in anthropology or archaeology. It assumes no field experience and no institutional access. The aim is conceptual literacy: what the discipline has learned about the human past, how archaeologists gather and date physical evidence, and how they use interpretive models to reconstruct social life from fragmented material remains.

The four subjects are meant to be studied in order:

- Start with Introduction to Archaeology for the history of the discipline, its ethical obligations toward cultural heritage, and how archaeological questions are formed.
- Next, Archaeological Methods and Field Techniques explains how sites are surveyed, excavated, recorded stratigraphically, and dated by relative and chronometric methods.
- Then Archaeological Theory gives you the frameworks for interpreting material patterns. It traces how the field moved from descriptive culture history to processual (scientific) and post-processual (contextual and critical) paradigms.
- Finish with World Prehistory, which applies the methods and theory to a global survey of early hominin evolution, the origins of agriculture, and the emergence of complex societies.

Prehistory and theoretical debates are hard to judge without knowing how field data is recovered, dated, and analyzed, so the sequence works best taken in order.

Note: This page covers only the essential core of undergraduate archaeology: the foundational concepts, methods, and narratives every learner should study before specializing. Once you have this baseline, you can move on to specialized tracks such as zooarchaeology, GIS and spatial modeling, or cultural resource management in [Advanced Topics](advanced_topics.md), or apply practical skills to concrete deliverables in [Projects](projects.md).

## Curriculum

### Introduction to Archaeology

An overview of archaeological principles, the development of the discipline from antiquarian collecting to modern anthropological science, and the ethical standards governing material heritage.

[Introduction to Archaeology Specialization (Coursera / Rice University)](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/introduction-to-archaeology): The main instructional spine for this section: an accessible four-course progression through archaeological history, scientific methodology, interpretation, and public ethics.

[Traces: An Open Introduction to Archaeology (LibreTexts / Society for Anthropology in Community Colleges)](https://socialsci.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Anthropology/Archaeology/Traces_-_An_Open_Introduction_to_Archaeology): A free, peer-reviewed open textbook. It works as a core reading companion to the Coursera course, or as a text-based path on its own for self-directed study.

[Intro to Archaeology (YouTube / Professor Brian Wygal)](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLsHH_OkB36-mRIj6T76nTARRqObkTY-Mf): An alternative video option with chapter-by-chapter classroom presentations aligned with Renfrew and Bahn's textbook. It suits visual learners who prefer standard lectures to the Coursera format.

[Archaeology: Theories, Methods, and Practice (Google Books / Colin Renfrew & Paul Bahn)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780500294246): The canonical benchmark textbook for undergraduate archaeology worldwide. Use it as an in-depth reference alongside the Wygal lectures, or as a comprehensive alternative to the open textbook.

### Archaeological Methods and Field Techniques

The practical procedures of archaeological research: reconnaissance, non-invasive survey, stratigraphic excavation, laboratory artifact analysis, and scientific dating.

[Field Manual for African Archaeology (Royal Museum for Central Africa / Alexandre Livingstone Smith et al.)](https://www.africamuseum.be/en/research/discover/publications/open-access/documents-social-sciences-humanities/fmaa): A comprehensive, openly downloadable field guide that serves as the core practical manual for this subject. It walks through project planning, field prospection, excavation procedures, and site recording.

[The Human Past: Introduction to Archaeology (MIT OpenCourseWare / Harry Merrick)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/3-986-the-human-past-introduction-to-archaeology-fall-2006): A university course whose lab modules and assignments complement the field manual with hands-on guidance in post-excavation analysis of stone tools, ceramics, archaeometallurgy, and faunal remains.

[Archaeological Dating (Crow Canyon Archaeological Center)](https://crowcanyon.org/education/learn-about-archaeology/archaeological-dating): A concise, beginner-friendly online tutorial on relative and chronometric dating. Read it as a primer before the more technical dating chapters in the field manual and the MIT course.

### Archaeological Theory

The main interpretive models archaeologists use to explain human culture and social change from material evidence, spanning culture history, processualism, and post-processual frameworks.

[Archaeological Theory: An Introduction (Google Books / Matthew Johnson)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781118475027): The standard, approachable survey of archaeological theory, meant to be studied after the introductory and methods units. For a free foundational alternative, review the theory chapters in the LibreTexts textbook or the Renfrew and Bahn volume listed in the introductory section.

### World Prehistory

A global synthesis of the human career from early hominin evolution and Paleolithic dispersals through the emergence of agriculture to the formation of ancient complex societies and states.

[People of the Earth: An Introduction to World Prehistory (Google Books / Brian Fagan & Nadia Durrani)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781138485614): The canonical introductory narrative text for global prehistory, tracing human development across every major world region. If buying a commercial textbook is a barrier, the lecture notes of the MIT course listed under Archaeological Methods and Field Techniques cover comparable milestones in agricultural origins and early state formation.
